Participation flow

How to take part

Five steps from registration to final demo. Everything after step 2 happens inside your team workspace, so keep your sign-in credentials safe.

  1. 01
    Register your team

    The team leader completes the form below with the team name, institution and chosen track. One registration per team — the leader's email becomes the team's login identity.

    Takes about 2 minutes · Free
  2. 02
    Confirm and sign in

    Your account is created immediately and a confirmation is recorded against your email. Sign in with the same credentials to unlock your private team workspace.

    Passwords are hashed by the identity provider and never stored in plain text
  3. 03
    Submit your idea brief

    Inside the workspace, file a one-page brief describing the problem, your approach and the dataset you intend to use. Briefs are reviewed on a rolling basis and you may revise yours until the screening deadline.

    Screening deadline: 5 days before the build window
  4. 04
    Build with mentor support

    Shortlisted teams enter the 36-hour build window. Your workspace connects you to the participant lounge and to a private desk shared only with the organising committee for review checkpoints.

    Live messaging, announcements and mentor reviews in one place
  5. 05
    Demo and evaluation

    Present a 10-minute working demo to the jury panel. Scoring weighs scientific validity, technical execution, reproducibility and real-world impact. Finalists are fast-tracked into our partner's hiring pipeline.

    Results announced on the closing day
Evaluation

What your idea submission must contain

Use this as a checklist before you file your brief from inside the workspace. Incomplete briefs are returned for revision rather than rejected.

Required sections
Roughly 400–600 words in total
  • Problem statement — the specific gap you are closing and who it affects.
  • Proposed approach — your method, model or assay, in enough detail to be judged.
  • Data & tooling — datasets, libraries and instruments you will rely on.
  • Expected outcome — what you will have working by the end of the build window.
  • Impact & feasibility — why it matters and why it is achievable in 36 hours.
  • Team roles — who owns which part of the work.
How the jury scores
100 points, applied identically to every track
  • Scientific validity — 30 Is the biology or chemistry sound?
  • Technical execution — 25 Does the build actually work end to end?
  • Reproducibility — 20 Can a reviewer rerun your results from your repository?
  • Real-world impact — 15 Who benefits, and at what scale?
  • Originality — 10 How far beyond an off-the-shelf solution does it go?
Work must be produced during the event. Pre-existing repositories may be referenced but not submitted as the entry.
